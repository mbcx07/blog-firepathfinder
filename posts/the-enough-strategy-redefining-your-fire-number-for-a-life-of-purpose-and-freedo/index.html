The 'Enough' Strategy: Redefining Your FIRE Number for a Life of Purpose and Freedom

2026-08-05 · FIRE Strategy · Tags: fire strategy, firepathfinder, The

When I first stumbled into the FIRE movement, I was captivated by the spreadsheets. The compound interest projections, the withdrawal rate formulas, the savings rate graphs—it all felt so beautifully mathematical. I spent hours tweaking my numbers, convinced that if I could just hit that magical seven-figure target, everything would fall into place. But as I got closer to my goal, a nagging question began to surface: What exactly is this number buying me? The answer, I realized, wasn't in the spreadsheet. It was in the life I wanted to live. This is the essence of what I call the 'Enough' strategy—a FIRE approach that prioritizes intentionality over raw accumulation. It’s not about finding the perfect number; it’s about defining what 'enough' means to you, and then building a plan that honors that definition.

The Myth of the Magic Number

Ask any FIRE enthusiast about their number, and you’ll likely hear a figure that follows the classic 25x rule—multiply your annual expenses by 25, and you’ve got your target. But this number, while useful as a starting point, is often a trap. It assumes that your current spending is both accurate and sustainable, and it ignores the fact that your life will inevitably change. Many people inflate their number because they’ve unconsciously absorbed the lifestyle creep of their peers or the fear of market downturns. They end up chasing a target that’s less about their actual needs and more about a vague sense of security.

The problem with the magic number is that it’s static, while your life is dynamic. A number calculated at age 30 may be wildly off by age 40, not just because of inflation, but because your values, health, and relationships have shifted. The 'Enough' strategy flips this script. Instead of starting with a number and working backward, you start with a vision of your ideal life and then calculate what that life would cost. This approach is both more personal and more realistic, because it forces you to confront the uncomfortable question: What do I actually want my days to look like?

The Three Dimensions of Enough

To define your 'enough,' you need to consider three distinct but interconnected dimensions: financial enough, time enough, and purpose enough. Most FIRE discussions focus almost exclusively on the first, but the other two are just as critical.

Financial enough is the most obvious—it’s the amount of money you need to cover your essential expenses, your discretionary spending, and the occasional splurge that brings genuine joy. But here’s the kicker: your financial enough is only meaningful if you’ve actually scrutinized your spending. A $5,000 monthly budget that includes a gym membership you never use, a car lease you don’t need, and a streaming subscription you forgot about is not 'enough'—it’s waste. Financial enough requires a ruthless audit of what truly adds value to your life.

Time enough is about how many years you’re willing to trade for money. Some people are perfectly content working until 55 because they love their job. Others would rather retire at 40 with a leaner budget. There’s no right answer, but you have to be honest about the trade-off. The 'Enough' strategy asks: How much of my finite time am I willing to spend on activities that don’t align with my core values? If you’re miserable in your current career, then a higher savings rate and an earlier retirement might be worth the temporary sacrifice. If you’re thriving, then perhaps you can afford to slow down and enjoy the journey.

Purpose enough is the dimension that most FIRE plans overlook. What will you do with your time once you’re no longer working? If you don’t have an answer, your retirement could quickly become a void. Purpose enough means identifying the activities, relationships, and contributions that give your life meaning—whether that’s volunteering, starting a hobby, or building a small business. Your FIRE number should be large enough to fund this purpose, but not so large that you delay it unnecessarily.

How to Calculate Your Personal Enough Number

Ready to ditch the generic 25x rule and craft a number that’s uniquely yours? Here’s a practical exercise that takes about an hour and will radically transform your FIRE plan.

Start by listing every expense you’ve had over the past three months. Categorize them into three buckets: essentials (housing, food, utilities, insurance), joyful extras (things that genuinely enhance your life—a gym you love, travel, dining out with friends), and mindless spending (subscriptions you rarely use, impulse purchases, convenience fees). Be brutally honest. That daily latte might be a joyful extra if you savor it, but if you’re just grabbing it out of habit, it’s mindless.

Next, for each item in the joyful extras bucket, ask yourself: Would I still be happy if this were doubled? Would I be devastated if it disappeared? This helps you separate fleeting pleasures from lasting satisfaction. Then, for the mindless bucket, calculate how much you could save by cutting or reducing these items. The goal isn’t to eliminate all fun—it’s to ensure that every dollar you spend is a dollar that reflects your values.

Once you have your adjusted monthly expenses, multiply by 12 to get your annual 'enough' spending. Then, apply a withdrawal rate that you’re comfortable with. If you’re retiring early, you might want to be more conservative—say 3.5% instead of 4%—to account for a longer retirement horizon. That gives you your personal enough number. But here’s the twist: don’t stop there. Write down the lifestyle that number supports. Describe your ideal day, your travel plans, your hobbies. If that description doesn’t excite you, your number is wrong—either too low because you’ve cut too much joy, or too high because you’re still clinging to societal expectations.

The Enough Mindset: Avoiding the Comparison Trap

One of the biggest obstacles to embracing the 'Enough' strategy is the constant bombardment of other people’s numbers. Social media is filled with FIRE influencers boasting about their $3 million portfolios or their 70% savings rates. It’s easy to feel like you’re falling behind. But here’s the truth: those numbers are as unique as fingerprints. What works for a single tech worker in San Francisco will never work for a family of four in rural Ohio—and that’s okay.

The comparison trap doesn’t just affect your savings rate; it also distorts your sense of enough. When you see someone retiring at 35, you might feel pressured to accelerate your own timeline, even if it means sacrificing the things you actually care about. The 'Enough' mindset requires a conscious effort to tune out the noise. Here are a few strategies that have helped me:

  • Unfollow accounts that make you feel inadequate. Curate your feed to include only those who inspire you without triggering envy.
  • Create a personal mission statement. Write down why you’re pursuing FIRE in the first place. When you feel the pull of comparison, read it aloud.
  • Celebrate small wins. Instead of fixating on the final number, acknowledge each milestone—whether it’s paying off a credit card or hitting a savings goal. This reinforces your own progress, not someone else’s.

Remember, the goal of FIRE isn’t to have the biggest number; it’s to have the most freedom. And freedom is inherently personal. What gives you freedom might look like a 4-day workweek, while someone else might need a fully passive income stream. Define your own finish line.

Adjusting Your Enough Over Time

Your 'enough' is not a one-and-done calculation. Life throws curveballs—a new child, a health scare, a career change, a sudden desire to move to the coast. Each of these events will shift your spending, your time horizon, and your sense of purpose. The 'Enough' strategy is a living document, not a static spreadsheet.

I recommend revisiting your enough number at least once a year, or whenever you experience a major life change. During these reviews, ask yourself three questions: Has my spending changed in a way that reflects my values? Am I still on track to retire when I want? And most importantly, does my current plan still make me excited about the future? If the answer to that last question is no, it’s time to recalibrate. Maybe you’ve discovered a passion for gardening that requires a bit more space, or you’ve decided to downsize and simplify. Adjust your number accordingly—not with guilt, but with intention.

One common pitfall is the tendency to let your enough number creep upward as your savings grow. This is the opposite of the lifestyle creep we’re trying to avoid. Just because you can afford a bigger house or a fancier car doesn’t mean you should. The 'Enough' strategy is about saying 'no' to what you don’t need so you can say 'yes' to what matters most. If you find your number inflating, go back to your joy audit and ask whether those new expenses truly add value or are just a response to external pressures.

The Power of Enough in Early Retirement

When you finally make the leap into early retirement, the 'Enough' strategy becomes your greatest ally. Without a clear sense of enough, you’ll likely fall into one of two traps: either you’ll overspend because you’re afraid of missing out, or you’ll underspend because you’re terrified of running out of money. Both scenarios undermine the freedom you worked so hard to achieve.

Having a well-defined enough number gives you confidence. You know exactly what your lifestyle costs, and you’ve built a portfolio that can sustain it. This confidence allows you to withdraw from your investments without guilt, because you’ve already accounted for the expenses that bring you joy. It also allows you to weather market downturns with equanimity, because your spending is aligned with your values, not with a fear-driven impulse to hoard.

Moreover, the 'Enough' strategy transforms retirement from a passive state of 'not working' into an active pursuit of purpose. When you’ve defined what enough means for you, you can allocate your time to the activities that matter most—whether that’s mentoring young professionals, writing a novel, or simply spending more time with loved ones. You’re not just living off your investments; you’re living into your values.

Conclusion

The FIRE movement often feels like a numbers game—a race to accumulate as much as possible, as quickly as possible. But the 'Enough' strategy reminds us that the real prize isn’t the number itself; it’s the life that number enables. By shifting your focus from abstract accumulation to intentional living, you can build a FIRE plan that is both financially sound and deeply fulfilling. Start by auditing your spending, defining your three dimensions of enough, and recalculating your number with purpose. Then, commit to revisiting that number as your life evolves. In doing so, you’ll discover that the freedom you seek isn’t on the other side of a spreadsheet—it’s in the clarity of knowing what truly matters to you. That’s the ultimate FIRE strategy, and it’s one that no calculator can provide.
